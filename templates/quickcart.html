UX case study

QuickCart

Designing a grocery-shopping app for people short on time.

Role
UX research & design
Context
Google UX Design capstone
Tools
Figma · Adobe XD
Format
App concept

The shopping problem

QuickCart explores grocery shopping for working adults who struggle to find time for an in-person trip. I designed a mobile shopping flow from browsing products to checkout.

This is a course design project and prototype; it was not released as a production app.

Research findings

  • Finding time to shop

    Interviews and empathy maps highlighted the difficulty of fitting grocery shopping around work.

  • Grocery quality

    People wanted confidence in the quality of groceries ordered online.

  • Text and viewing needs

    Different text and viewing needs influenced the design considerations.

Changes after feedback

I started with low-fidelity wireframes, then developed a more detailed prototype. Usability feedback informed changes including recent activity and bottom navigation.

Prototype walkthrough

A walkthrough of the original interactive concept, from browsing to checkout.

Visual demo: navigating the grocery app, selecting products, reviewing the cart, and proceeding through checkout.

What I took from it

QuickCart taught me to work out how someone moves through a design before refining how it looks. Feedback on the shopping flow led me to rethink navigation and make recently viewed items easier to reach.

I carried that approach into earlier versions of my portfolio and this website: putting the work first, simplifying navigation, and making it easier to move from a project preview into the details. I’m still applying the same habit—review the experience, make a focused change, and check it again.
